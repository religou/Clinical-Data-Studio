# Sites That Under or Overreport Events Detection Methodology

## Sites That Under or Overreport Events Detection Methodology
 The Under-Over Reporting Events Plot visualizes event   reporting patterns across adverse events (AE), concomitant medication (CM), and medical history (MH) at the site and country level. 
 To determine sites and countries that are over or underreporting events, RBQM first performs the following event rate calculations at the site and country level: 
- Adverse Events (AE) Rate – The average number of adverse events reported per exposure time. Calculated as: N umber of AEs / Exposure Time in Months
 Note : Exposure time is the number of study days across all patients.
- Concomitant Medication (CM) Rate – The average number of reported concomitant medications per unit of reported adverse events and ongoing medical history events reported. Calculated as:  Number of CMs / (Number of AEs + Number of Ongoing MH) 
- Medical History (MH) Rate – The average number of reported medical history events per enrolled patient at the site or country. Calculated as: N umber of MH / Total Patients
 RBQM then identifies sites and countries with outliers in reported AE, CM, and MH rates. For this,   reported events are assumed to be events in a Poisson distribution. Hence, reported events are compared to the expected events under Poisson Continuous Distributed Functions (CDF) based on which p-value are computed. Then, RBQM calculates the  95% and 99% confidence interval limit  for each rate under the different aggregations. 
 The Under-Over Reporting Events Plot then visualizes event   reporting patterns and identifies sites under or overreport events using three separate funnel plots :
- Adverse Events (AE) – Graphs the reported rate of adverse events over time in months for each site and country in the study. 
- Concomitant Medication (CM) – Graphs the reported rate of concomitant medications compared to the combined number of reported adverse events and ongoing medical history events for each site and country in the study. 
- Medical History (MH) – Graphs the reported rate of medical history events compared to total number of enrolled patients at each site and country in the study.

 Each site and country's reported data is represented as a blue data point on each graph. RBQM normalizes the study-wide reported event data and visually outputs funnel lines representing the data's:
- Upper 99% Limit – An AE rate that is at the 99% confidence interval above the study's average AE rate at a particular time in the study. RBQM considers sites or countries whose reported AE rate lies above the upper 99% limit to potentially be extreme outliers. Extreme outliers may warrant prompt investigation to  understand the type of events that are overreported; for example, signs and symptoms versus diagnosis . The Upper 99% Limit is represented by the uppermost solid, blue line in the funnel plot: 
- Upper 95% Limit – An AE rate that is at the 95% confidence interval  above the study's average AE rate at a particular time in the study. RBQM considers sites or countries whose reported AE rate lies above the upper 95% limit to be potential outliers. Outliers may warrant investigation to understand the type of events that are overreported; for example, signs and symptoms versus diagnosis . The Upper 95% Limit is r epresented by the uppermost dotted, gray line in the funnel plot: 
- Mean – The average reported AE rate across all sites or countries in the study. The mean is represented by the solid, yellow, middle line in the funnel plot: 
- Lower 95% Limit – An AE rate that is at the 95% confidence interval  below the study's average AE rate at a particular time in the study. RBQM considers sites or countries whose reported AE rate lies below the lower 95% limit to be potential outliers. Outliers may warrant investigation to understand type of events that are underreported; for example, mild events . The Lower 95% Limit is r epresented by the lowermost dotted, gray line in the funnel plot: 
- Lower 99% Limit – An AE rate that is at the 99% confidence interval below the study's average AE rate at a particular time in the study. RBQM considers sites or countries whose reported AE rate lies below the lower 99% limit to potentially be extreme outliers. Extreme outliers may warrant prompt investigation  to understand the type of events that are underreported; for example, mild events .
