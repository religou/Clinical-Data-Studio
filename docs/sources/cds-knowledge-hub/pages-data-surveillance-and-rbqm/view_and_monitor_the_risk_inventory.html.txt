# View and Monitor the Risk Inventory

## View and Monitor the Risk Inventory
 You can use the Risk Inventory to view all:
- Risks documented as part of your study's risk assessment, as well as associated risk and risk review details. 
- QTLs associated with a risk in your study, as well as how well the QTLs are performing. 
- KRIs associated with a risk in your study, as well as the KRIs' current KRI values. 
 You can also use the Risk Inventory to monitor and track the risk score of each study risk. Doing so enables your team to:  
- Better understand which risks pose the biggest threat to the study outcome. 
- Prioritize the risks in which you develop risk mitigation strategies for. 
- Track risks to monitor whether the risk level is increasing or decreasing. 
- Determine the effectiveness of risk mitigation strategies and whether or not additional or adjusted mitigations are needed. 
 To learn more, see Risk Scores .
## View and Monitor Study Risks, Risk Details, and Risk Reviews
 You can use the Risk Inventory to view the list of all risks documented as part of your study's risk assessment, as well as associated risk and risk review details. For example, you can: 
- View a risk's status; for example, whether the risk is open or closed. 
- View risks whose risk reviews are overdue or due soon. 
- Sort the Risk Inventory based on risk score to help prioritize the risks that your team monitors and addresses. 
 Data for the highest study environment level appears by default.

 Tip: Study Total Risk Score
 The upper right of the Risk Inventory page shows the Study Total Risk Score , which is the combined total risk score of all risks in your study designated with the risk status of Open or Watch . The Study Total Risk Score can give you a general sense of the study's overall level of risk.

## Filter the Risks Table
 To help find the risks you need, you can filter the Risks table to show:
- Specific risks by risk name or risk origin. 
- Only risks that are currently in a specific review status, such as Overdue or Due Soon. 
- Only risks that are currently designated with specific risk status, such as Open, Closed, or Watch. 
- Only risks that do or do not require action.

 To help find the risks you need, you can sort the Risks table's columns vertically in ascending or descending alphanumeric order. For example, you can sort to more quickly find a specific risk by Risk ID. Or you can sort by the Target Review Date to see which risks' review are most overdue or nearest the due date. You can also s ort the Risks table by the Risk Score column to show the highest risk scores at the top to view and prioritize risks that may most require mitigation.

## View QTLs Associated with Risks and Evaluate QTL Performance
 QTLs are study-level metrics that indicate if a study parameter's reported data has breached a preidentified data quality threshold or upper limit . QTLs monitor the emergence of systematic errors on critical variables and data that may be prone to subjective bias, ultimately impacting: 
- Patient safety 
- Study conduct and performance 
- Compliance 
- Data quality 
- Study reliability 
 You can view the list of all QTLs associated with a risk in your study as well as each  QTL's details: 
- For oversight to ensure the study team is following the risk plan 
- As a reference for another study's risk assessment. 
- The view each QTL's current observed value. 
- To view the current risk score of the risk that each QTL is associated with. 
 Tip : You can also edit a QTL's details. For example, you can: 
- Update the risk name or description in care of entry error, typo, or for additional clarity. 
- Manually update the QTL's expectation , observed value, threshold , and upper limit if the QTL is not integrated with the rest of RBQM. 
- Document QTL deviations. 
 To learn more, see  View and Edit the Details of QTLs Associated with Risks and Document QTL Deviations .
 To learn more about QTLs, see About Quality Tolerance Limits .

 | Observed Value 
 | 
 The current observed percentage of patients that do not meet the study parameter's criteria as of the most recent data analysis. 
 A green value indicates that the percentage of patients that do not meet the study parameter's specified criteria is considered within a healthy range of the expectation . Generally, in range QTLs require no action. 
 A yellow value indicates that the percentage of patients that do not meet the study parameter's specified criteria have exceeded the tolerance threshold . RBQM considers parameters whose QTL values exceed the threshold as at risk. This indicates potential underlying systemic issues, which can compromise study safety and integrity. You should investigate such parameters to determine the root cause and to prevent or mitigate study issues. 
 A red value indicates that the percentage of patients that do not meet the study parameter's specified criteria has exceeded the tolerance upper limit . RBQM considers parameters whose QTL values exceed the upper limit as high risk. This indicates potential underlying systemic issues, which can compromise study safety and integrity. You should investigate such parameters to determine the root cause and to prevent or mitigate study issues. 
 A red value and a fully red bar indicate a QTL excursion. RBQM  flags and identifies QTL excursions when : 
- The percentage of patients that do not meet the study parameter's specified criteria has exceeded the tolerance upper limit, and 
- It is impossible to move the QTL value back within a healthy range of the QTL's expected value no matter the actions taken by the project team. 
 QTL excursions indicate that the study quality is compromised to the point that the study may need to be shut down or restarted.

## View KRIs Associated with Risks and Evaluate KRI Performance
 Key Risk Indicators (KRIs) are predefined study metrics that enable you for the duration of the study to systematically:  
- Identify and track known or anticipated risks to data quality. 
- Evaluate country and site performance in a study. 
- Ensure study conduct operates as planned. 
 Examples of KRIs include patient accrual rates and data query rates.
 You can track KRI performance using KRI values . KRI value calculations reflect as a percentage, count, mean, rate, or standard deviation. 
 You can view all key risk indicators (KRIs) associated with a risk in your study as well as each KRI's details: 
- For oversight to ensure the study team is following the risk plan. 
- As a reference for another study's risk assessment. 
- To view the KRIs' latest recorded KRI values. 
- To view the current risk score of the risk that each KRI is associated with. 
 Tip: You can also edit a KRI's detail. For example, you can: 
- Update the KRI name or description in care of entry error, typo, or for additional clarity. 
- Update the KRI's value. 
 To learn more, see  View and Edit the Details of KRIs Associated with Risks . 
 To learn more about KRIs, see About Key Risk Indicators .
