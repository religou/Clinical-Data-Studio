# Risk Score Methodology

## Risk Score Methodology
 Risk Assessment calculates the risk score for: 
- The entire study, also known as the Study Total Risk Score. 
- All combined risk categories included the Risk Assessment, also known as the Total Risk Categorization Score. 
- Individual risk categories, also known as the Risk Category Score. 
- Individual risks, as known as Risk Scores. 
 Each type of risk score calculation is independent of the others and uses different calculation methodologies. 
## Study Total Risk Score
 The Study Total Risk Score shows at the top of the Risk Inventory page. 
 Study Total Risk Score = The combined total risk score of all risks in your study designated with the risk status of Open or Watch 
 Risks designated with the risk status of Closed or Transfer do not get tallied toward the study total risk score. The study total risk score can give you a general sense of the study's overall level of risk. 
## Total Risk Categorization Score
 The Total Risk Categorization Score shows at the top of the Risk Assessment > Risk Categorization page. 
 Total Risk Categorization Score = The combined total risk category score of all risk categories included in the Risk Assessment 
 For example: 
- 10 risk categories with the following risk category scores: 10 + 12 + 8 + 10 + 6 + 4 + 15 + 15 +10 + 10. 
- The sum of the risk scores for all categories equals 100. 
 The Total Risk Categorization Score uses a color-coded meter to indicate the study's level of risk. Green indicates minimal risk; yellow indicates moderate risk; red indicates high risk. The color associated with the score is calculated by dividing the Total Risk Categorization Score by the number of risk categories x 27. Risk Assessment then converts the result to a percentage. In the example above, the percentage that determines color calculates as 100/(10 x 27) = 0.37 or 37%.  
 Risk Assessment shows the color of the Total Risk Categorization Score as Yellow based on the percentage: 
- 0-32% (Green)  
- 33%-66% (Yellow) 
- 67%-100% (Red)

## Risk Category Score
 Risk Assessment calculates the risk category score for each risk category based on the following risk factors: Impact, Detectability , Probability.  
 Risk Category Score = (Probability x Impact x Detectability) x Risk Category Weight 
 On a 3-point risk score scale, the maximum risk score is 27. Color coding indicates the severity of the risk where values 0.1-3.0 appear in green (minimal risk), 3.1-9.0 appear in yellow (moderate risk), and 9.1-27.0 appear in red (high risk). On a 5-point risk score scale, the maximum risk score is 125. Color coding indicates the severity of risk where values 0.1-5.0 appear in green (minimal risk), 5.1-25.0 appear in yellow (moderate risk), and 25.1-125.0 appear in red (high risk). 
 Risk category scores enable your team to: 
- Better understand which risk categories pose the biggest threat to the study outcome. 
- Prioritize the risk categories in which you develop risk mitigation strategies for. 
- Track risk categories to monitor whether their risk level is increasing or decreasing. 
- Determine the effectiveness of risk mitigation strategies and whether or not additional or adjusted mitigations are needed. 
 To learn more, see:
- Score a Risk Category's Level of Risk to the Study 
- Assign a Risk Score Weight to a Risk Category 
 Tip : Risk Assessment tallies the collective sum of each risk category's score to determine the Total Risk Categorization Score. To learn more, see Total Risk Categorization Score . 
## Risk Scores
 You can identify and add to the risk assessment the study risks associated with critical data, critical processes, and risk categories.  
 When logging risks, you can s core each risk in your study by assessing the probability, impact, and detectability of the risk on a 3- or 5-point scale (depending on your study's configuration. This score is known as the risk score . Risk Assessment calculates the risk score based on the following risk factors: 
- Impact — Indicates the amount of impact to the clinical trial if the risk becomes an issue.
- On a 3-point risk score scale:  High impact = 3, Medium impact = 2, Low impact = 1. 
- On a 5-point risk score scale:  High impact = 5, Medium impact = 3, Low impact = 1. 
- Probability — Indicates the likelihood that a risk becomes an issue.
- On a 3-point risk score scale:  High probability = 3, Medium probability = 2, Low probability = 1. 
- On a 5-point risk score scale:  High probability = 5, Medium probability = 3, Low probability = 1. 
- Detectability — Indicates how difficult it is to detect a risk before it becomes a systemic issue. A low detectability score usually means lower risk.
- On a 3-point risk score scale:  Difficult to Detect = 3, Medium to Detect = 2, Easy to Detect = 1.
- On a 5-point risk score scale:  Difficult to Detect = 5, Medium to Detect = 3, Easy to Detect = 1.
 Risk Score = (Probability x Impact x Detectability) 
 Risk Assessment automatically multiplies the Probability, Impact, and Detectability risk factor scores to generate the overall risk score. On a 3-point risk score scale, the maximum risk score is 27. Color coding indicates the severity of risk where values 1-3 appear in green (minimal risk), 4-9 appear in yellow (moderate risk), and 10-27 appear in red (high risk). On a 5-point risk score scale, the maximum risk score is 125. Color coding indicates the severity of risk where values 1-5 appear in green (minimal risk), 6-25 appear in yellow (moderate risk), and 26-125 appear in red (high risk). 
 Risk scores enable your team to: 
- Better understand which risks pose the biggest threat to the study outcome. 
- Prioritize the risks in which you develop risk mitigation strategies for. 
- Track risks to monitor whether the risk level is increasing or decreasing. 
- Determine the effectiveness of risk mitigation strategies and whether or not additional or adjusted mitigations are needed.
